## 中式英语问题

1. **With the deepening of...** — 这是最典型的 Chinglish 开头。英文不用 With the deepening of 引出背景，改用 As China's urbanization accelerates / As Chinese cities continue to expand

2. **but this way is very expensive and hard to construct** — "this way" 指代模糊；"hard to construct" 是字面翻译，应用 costly and technically demanding

3. **will disappear forever** — forever 在这里过于口语化且绝对，应用 permanently / irreversibly

---

## 句法拆解

原文第 2 句是一个长句，包含"做 A → 导致 B；做 C → 但代价高"的对比逻辑：

> 一些城市选择了大规模拆除重建，结果导致城市面貌千篇一律；另一些城市则尝试在保留原有建筑风貌的基础上融入现代功能，但这一做法成本高昂且施工难度大。

标准拆分方案：
- **主句 1**: Some cities have opted for wholesale demolition and reconstruction,
- **结果**: only to find their cityscapes becoming indistinguishable from one another.
- **主句 2**: Others have attempted to integrate modern functions while preserving original architectural character —
- **转折**: an approach that is both costly and technically demanding.

关键技巧：用 "only to find" 表达意外结果；用破折号引出对比；用 "architectural character" 而非 "building style"

## 标准译文

As China's urbanization has accelerated, many cities have found themselves confronting a shared dilemma: how to preserve historic districts while accommodating the demands of modern development. Some have opted for large-scale demolition and reconstruction, only to see their cityscapes reduced to homogenized anonymity. Others have attempted to integrate modern functions while retaining the character of the original architecture — an approach that is both costly and technically demanding. Scholars have noted that the value of historic districts lies not only in the buildings themselves, but in the collective memory and cultural identity they embody — intangible assets that, once erased, are lost permanently.

## 高分译文

The deepening of China's urbanization has presented many cities with a persistent tension: the imperative to preserve historic quarters versus the pressure to modernize. Cities that have pursued wholesale demolition and reconstruction have often ended up with what critics describe as "cookie-cutter" cityscapes — visually interchangeable and stripped of local identity. Those that have tried to thread the needle — retaining period architecture while weaving in modern functionality — have found the process prohibitively expensive and technically exacting. As some scholars have argued, the value of a historic district resides less in its bricks and mortar than in the collective memory and cultural identity it embodies. These are the invisible assets that, once dismantled, cannot be recovered.
