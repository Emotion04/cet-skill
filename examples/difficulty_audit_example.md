# Difficulty Audit Example

以下展示一个 CET-6 仔细阅读 Passage 从**偏简单 → 达标**的修正过程，作为生成 CET-6 题目时的难度校准参考。

---

## 场景

用户要求：六级，仔细阅读。

---

## ❌ 修正前（偏简单，相当于 CET-4 难度）

### Passage One

The Impact of Remote Work on Urban Development

In recent years, remote work has become increasingly common around the world. Many companies now allow their employees to work from home, which has changed how cities function. This trend has both positive and negative effects on urban development.

First, remote work reduces traffic congestion. When fewer people commute to offices every day, there are fewer cars on the roads. This leads to less air pollution and shorter travel times for those who still need to commute. Cities like San Francisco and London have reported significant drops in rush-hour traffic since the shift to remote work began.

Second, remote work changes the demand for office space. Many companies have reduced their office footprints, which has led to lower commercial real estate prices in some areas. However, this also means that businesses that depend on office workers, such as coffee shops and restaurants, have seen fewer customers.

Third, remote work allows people to live farther from city centers. When workers do not need to commute daily, they can choose to live in smaller towns or rural areas where housing is more affordable. This could help reduce overcrowding in major cities.

In conclusion, remote work has changed urban development in significant ways. While there are challenges, the overall trend appears to be beneficial for both workers and the environment.

### 为什么太简单？

| 问题 | 诊断 |
|------|------|
| 文章结构 | 经典"五段式"：topic sentence + first/second/third + in conclusion，过于整洁 |
| 词汇 | 几乎全是 CET-4 基础词汇，没有 CET-6 级词（如 exacerbate, spatial, exacerbate, redistribution） |
| 句长 | 平均 ~18 词，没有超过 25 词的句子 |
| 推理层 | 全部 L1 同义改写，没有 L2/L3 推理题："main idea" 选项 B 直接重复首段句子 |
| 干扰项 | 大部分 G1/G2 级：46A "only positive" 一眼排除；48C "no longer profitable" 明显极端 |
| 作者态度 | 50 题直接选 "generally positive"，文章最后一句已经写了 "appears to be beneficial" |

## ✅ 修正后（CET-6 难度）

### Passage One

When nearly half of Manhattan's office workers abandoned their commutes in the spring of 2020, the immediate assumption was that the change would be temporary. Office towers would fill again, subway ridership would rebound, and the lunch-hour economy would resume its familiar rhythm. But as remote and hybrid arrangements have persisted beyond the initial disruption, researchers have begun to detect a more durable reorganization of urban geography — one whose consequences are less straightforward than early predictions suggested.

The most visible shift has been the hollowing out of downtown business districts during weekdays. Office vacancy rates in cities such as San Francisco and Chicago have climbed to historic highs, and the assessed value of commercial real estate has fallen sharply in several metropolitan cores. This might suggest a straightforward decline, yet the same data reveals a more complex pattern: while central business districts have suffered, residential neighborhoods within the same cities — particularly those with access to parks, good schools, and local retail — have seen property values rise. The "death of the city" narrative, it turns out, masks a redistribution rather than a collapse.

That redistribution extends beyond real estate. Spending that once concentrated in downtown lunch spots and after-work bars has partly dispersed to neighborhood cafes and suburban retail. Some service workers have benefited from jobs that moved closer to where they live; others have lost employment entirely as foot traffic patterns shifted. The net effect on urban employment, researchers at the University of Chicago's Urban Labs have argued, is ambiguous: aggregate job numbers have remained relatively stable, but the composition and location of those jobs have changed in ways that do not favor all workers equally.

Perhaps the least examined consequence concerns public transit. With ridership still below pre-shift levels, many transit agencies face steep budget shortfalls. Some have proposed cutting service on commuter routes while increasing investment in neighborhood connectors — a strategy that would, in effect, redesign transit networks around the geography of remote work. Whether this constitutes an adaptation or a capitulation is a matter of ongoing debate among urban policy scholars. What is clear is that the relationship between where people work, where they live, and how they move between the two has been altered — and that the full implications of this alteration remain unsettled.

### Questions

46. The author uses the phrase "redistribution rather than a collapse" (Paragraph 2) to suggest that:
A) downtown business districts will eventually recover their previous value
B) the decline in central business districts has been balanced by growth elsewhere
C) property values have fallen equally across all parts of cities
D) the shift to remote work is only temporary

47. According to Paragraph 3, the effect of remote work on urban employment has been:
A) uniformly negative for service workers
B) clearly beneficial for most employees
C) mixed, with uneven gains and losses across groups
D) negligible, since total job numbers did not change

48. In discussing public transit (Paragraph 4), the author notes a tension between:
A) reducing fares and maintaining service quality
B) cutting commuter routes and redesigning networks for new patterns
C) increasing taxes and investing in infrastructure
D) supporting remote workers and encouraging office returns

49. The word "capitulation" (Paragraph 4) is closest in meaning to:
A) negotiation
B) surrender
C) innovation
D) regulation

50. Which of the following best describes the author's overall perspective on the urban changes discussed?
A) alarmed by the economic damage to downtown districts
B) convinced that the changes will ultimately prove beneficial
C) interested in the complexity and unresolved nature of the changes
D) skeptical that remote work will remain a permanent feature

### Answer Key (生成时隐藏此部分，用户作答后揭示)

46. B — 推理题 L2：需综合理解第二段 "hollowing out" + "residential neighborhoods... have seen property values rise" + 明确句 "masks a redistribution rather than a collapse"
47. C — 细节改写 L1/L2：对应 "ambiguous" + "do not favor all workers equally"
48. B — 细节概括 L2：对应 "cutting service on commuter routes while increasing investment in neighborhood connectors"
49. B — 词义题 L1：句式对比 "adaptation or a capitulation" 暗示反义
50. C — 主旨/态度题 L3：全文未给出定论，最后一句 "remain unsettled" 是关键

## 修正前后对比

| 维度 | 修正前 | 修正后 |
|------|--------|--------|
| 文章结构 | 五段式教科书体 | 四段式分析体，无缝衔接过渡 |
| 词汇难度 | CET-4 基础词为主 | 含 CET-6 级词（spatial/durable/hollowing/reorganization/redistribution/aggregate/capitulation） |
| 平均句长 | ~18 词 | ~24 词 |
| 从句层级 | 1–2 层 | 2–4 层嵌套 |
| 推理深度 | 全部 L1 | L1/L2/L3 混合分布 |
| 干扰项质量 | G1/G2（一眼排除） | G3/G4（需仔细比对），50 题含 G5 |
| 作者立场 | 首段明说 "positive and negative"，末段说 "beneficial" | 全文中立分析，最后"remain unsettled"不给定论 |
| 文本真实感 | AI 作文 | 接近 The Atlantic/CityLab 风格 |

## 使用说明

生成 CET-6 题目时，模型应：
1. 先阅读修正前的版本，理解"什么是太简单"；
2. 再参照修正后的版本，理解目标质量；
3. 生成时完成难度锚点（3 个）和生成后自检清单。
